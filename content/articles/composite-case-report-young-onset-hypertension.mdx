## Introduction

Hypertension diagnosed before age 40, particularly when severe or resistant to initial therapy, warrants a broader differential diagnosis than essential (primary) hypertension. This composite case illustrates a structured approach to evaluating young-onset hypertension, organized using the standard case-report format for educational clarity.

## Patient Information

A composite patient, "J," is presented as a 27-year-old with no prior diagnosis of hypertension, found to have elevated blood pressure on a routine visit. Relevant composite history includes no known family history of hypertension, no current medications, and no reported illicit substance use.

## Clinical Findings

On the composite presentation, blood pressure was elevated on two separate readings, with mild hypokalemia reported on basic metabolic panel and no other localizing findings on physical examination.

## Diagnostic Assessment

Young-onset hypertension with unexplained hypokalemia should prompt consideration of secondary causes, particularly primary aldosteronism, renal artery stenosis (especially fibromuscular dysplasia in a younger patient), and, less commonly, pheochromocytoma or Cushing syndrome.

The aldosterone-to-renin ratio is a common first-line screening test for primary aldosteronism:

$$
\text{ARR} = \frac{\text{Plasma Aldosterone Concentration}}{\text{Plasma Renin Activity}}
$$

An elevated ratio, particularly with suppressed renin, raises suspicion for autonomous aldosterone production and generally prompts confirmatory testing before imaging.

| Consideration | Supporting Feature in This Composite | Typical Next Step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary aldosteronism | Hypokalemia, young age | Aldosterone-to-renin ratio |
| Renal artery stenosis (FMD) | Young age, no other risk factors | Renal artery imaging |
| Pheochromocytoma | Would expect paroxysmal symptoms (not present here) | Plasma metanephrines if suspected |

## Therapeutic Intervention

In this composite scenario, confirmatory testing was consistent with primary aldosteronism, and subtype evaluation directed management. Depending on subtype (unilateral adenoma versus bilateral hyperplasia), management in the literature ranges from surgical adrenalectomy to mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist therapy such as spironolactone.

## Follow-up and Outcomes

In this illustrative composite, blood pressure and potassium normalized over several months of targeted therapy, consistent with patterns described in published case series of appropriately treated primary aldosteronism.

## Discussion

This composite case illustrates why young-onset or hypokalemia-associated hypertension merits a broader secondary workup rather than default initiation of standard first-line antihypertensives alone. Missing a secondary, potentially correctable cause has both clinical and long-term risk implications.
