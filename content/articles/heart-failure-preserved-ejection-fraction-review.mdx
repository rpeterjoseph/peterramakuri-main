Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) now accounts for roughly half of all heart failure diagnoses, yet for decades it lacked an evidence-based pharmacologic treatment comparable to those available for heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF). This review outlines why HFpEF is mechanistically distinct and summarizes the trial evidence that has changed its management over the past several years.

## A Syndrome, Not a Single Disease

HFpEF is defined clinically by signs and symptoms of heart failure in the presence of a left ventricular ejection fraction generally at or above 50%, together with evidence of diastolic dysfunction or elevated filling pressures. Unlike HFrEF, where impaired systolic contraction is the dominant abnormality, HFpEF is characterized by impaired ventricular relaxation and increased myocardial stiffness.

<Callout type="educational" title="Diastolic vs. Systolic Dysfunction">
In systolic dysfunction, the heart struggles to contract and eject blood. In diastolic dysfunction, the heart contracts adequately but the ventricle is too stiff to fill properly at normal pressures, so filling pressures rise to compensate — often producing pulmonary congestion despite a "normal" ejection fraction.
</Callout>

Patients with HFpEF are frequently older, more often female, and carry a high burden of comorbidities including hypertension, obesity, atrial fibrillation, and type 2 diabetes. This comorbidity burden is now considered central to disease pathophysiology rather than incidental to it.

## The Comorbidity-Driven Model

A widely cited model proposes that systemic, comorbidity-driven inflammation causes coronary microvascular endothelial dysfunction, which in turn reduces nitric oxide bioavailability to adjacent cardiomyocytes. Reduced cyclic GMP signaling in myocytes is associated with hypertrophy, increased resting tension, and interstitial fibrosis — the structural basis of a stiff, poorly compliant ventricle.

This model helps explain why HFpEF has historically resisted therapies that succeeded in HFrEF: many of those agents (ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, ARBs) target neurohormonal activation from impaired systolic pump function, a mechanism that is not the primary driver in HFpEF.

## Diagnostic Approach

Diagnosis typically combines natriuretic peptide levels, echocardiographic assessment of diastolic function, and, where uncertainty remains, invasive hemodynamic testing (often with exercise) to demonstrate elevated filling pressures. Several validated scoring systems (such as the H2FPEF score) assist in estimating pretest probability in patients with unexplained dyspnea and a preserved ejection fraction.

## Emerging Pharmacologic Evidence

For many years, no pharmacologic therapy convincingly reduced hard clinical outcomes in HFpEF. That changed with trials of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors.

| Trial | Agent | Population | Primary Outcome Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EMPEROR-Preserved | Empagliflozin | HFpEF (EF > 40%) | Reduced cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalization |
| DELIVER | Dapagliflozin | HFpEF/HFmrEF | Reduced cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure |

<Callout type="clinical" title="Practice Implication">
SGLT2 inhibitors are now recommended across the ejection fraction spectrum in most major heart failure guidelines, representing one of the few drug classes with consistent benefit in both HFrEF and HFpEF. This is a meaningful shift from the historical absence of effective HFpEF-specific pharmacotherapy.
</Callout>

Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists and, in selected patients, the angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitor class have shown more modest or subgroup-specific benefit, and are generally considered adjunctive rather than foundational therapy in current guidelines.

## Summary

HFpEF is best understood as a systemic, comorbidity-driven syndrome of ventricular stiffness rather than a mirror image of HFrEF. The introduction of SGLT2 inhibitors marks the first pharmacologic class with robust, consistent outcome benefit in this population, though management otherwise remains heavily focused on treating contributing comorbidities individually.
